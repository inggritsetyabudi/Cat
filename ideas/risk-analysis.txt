C@ Risk Analysis

1. Design Decisions

Risk 1: Ownership model still feels complex
Cause
- Even simplified affine systems can overwhelm users if diagnostics are weak.

Impact
- Adoption slowdown
- Misuse of raw blocks as escape hatches

Mitigation
- Keep only two view kinds
- Ban user lifetime syntax
- Invest heavily in ownership diagnostics and IDE explanations
- Provide standard refactoring patterns for move/view fixes

Risk 2: Syntax uniqueness harms onboarding
Cause
- Deliberately non-derivative syntax reduces immediate familiarity.

Impact
- Early resistance from experienced developers

Mitigation
- Keep grammar small and consistent
- Use plain words instead of symbolic novelty
- Ship formatter, docs, and examples early

Risk 3: Checked integer semantics hurt hot-path performance
Cause
- `Int*` overflow checks may survive optimization in some code paths.

Impact
- Performance regressions relative to low-level languages

Mitigation
- Separate `Bits*` for wrapping arithmetic
- Allow optimizer removal of proven-safe checks
- Provide profiling guidance and low-level numeric APIs

Risk 4: FFI boundary weakens safety story
Cause
- Production systems require interop with unsafe external libraries.

Impact
- Real-world bugs may cluster at wrappers

Mitigation
- Raw boundary remains explicit
- Safe wrappers mandatory for public APIs
- Linter flags expansive raw regions
- Conformance suite includes FFI ownership tests

Risk 5: Compiler implementation complexity
Cause
- Strong ownership diagnostics, incremental compilation, and multi-stage IR are costly to build.

Impact
- Slow delivery
- unstable early tooling

Mitigation
- Freeze semantics before IR
- Build AIR/OIR/LIR in sequence
- keep v1 feature set narrow
- prioritize compiler test infrastructure early

Risk 6: Ecosystem immaturity
Cause
- New language lacks packages, docs, and community patterns.

Impact
- Limited production adoption despite strong core

Mitigation
- Ship batteries-included stdlib
- Provide official tooling end to end
- target infrastructure niches first where value is strongest

Risk 7: Pressure to add shortcuts that violate the core
Cause
- Users will request macros, hidden propagation, implicit conversions, and relaxed ownership.

Impact
- Conceptual drift
- contradiction with master blueprint

Mitigation
- ideas.txt remains authoritative
- edition policy gates changes
- new feature review requires FAST/SAFE/SIMPLE proof and cross-file consistency check

2. Rationale

FAST
- Risks are analyzed not only for correctness but for deployment cost and performance regressions.

SAFE
- The highest-risk area is boundary unsafety, especially raw and foreign interop.

SIMPLE
- Complexity creep is treated as a first-class design risk.

3. Constraints

- Risks may not be “solved” by weakening safety.
- No mitigation may contradict ideas.txt.
- Performance complaints do not justify undefined behavior.
- Familiarity complaints do not justify syntax collapse into existing-language imitation.

4. Trade-offs

- The design accepts some early friction to preserve a coherent long-term core.
- Strict governance reduces experimentation speed, but protects language integrity.
- Production credibility depends more on consistency than on rapid feature expansion.
